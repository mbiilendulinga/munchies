 Munchies | Analysis Insights

POWER BI : https://www.mbiile.com/projects/munchies/

This document records the main observations from the Munchies business performance analysis.

The primary analysis period is 2023–2025. The database contains partial records through February 2026, but these are excluded from the main year-on-year comparisons.

---

 1. Revenue Performance

Revenue increased consistently across the three complete years.

| Year |     Revenue | YoY Growth |
| ---- | ----------: | ---------: |
| 2023 | ZMW 362.98M |          — |
| 2024 | ZMW 508.77M |      40.2% |
| 2025 | ZMW 799.65M |      57.2% |

Revenue increased by approximately 120.3% between 2023 and 2025.

The monthly trend shows that the higher annual revenue in 2025 was not caused by a single isolated month. Most months in 2025 operated at a substantially higher revenue level than the corresponding months in 2023.

The highest monthly revenue in the reporting period was recorded in November 2025 at ZMW 77.18M, followed by December 2025 at ZMW 76.94M.

 7. Production Cost and Downtime

Production cost per unit remained relatively stable:

| Year | Production Cost |  Units | Cost per Unit |
| ---- | --------------: | -----: | ------------: |
| 2023 |     ZMW 767.50M | 27.95M |     ZMW 27.46 |
| 2024 |     ZMW 770.65M | 27.90M |     ZMW 27.62 |
| 2025 |     ZMW 760.82M | 27.66M |     ZMW 27.51 |

Despite changes in total production cost, the calculated cost per unit remained around ZMW 27.5.

Downtime varied more significantly month to month. January 2025 recorded the highest monthly downtime at approximately 369.9 hours.

The dataset supports comparison between downtime and production output, but does not by itself establish a causal relationship between the two.

 8. Inventory

Monthly closing stock remained broadly within the range of approximately 14.3M to 18.3M units during 2023–2025.

The highest monthly closing stock in the supplied trend occurred in March 2024 at 18.27M units.

Monthly stock value generally ranged between approximately ZMW 391M and ZMW 493M.

The inventory table also contains warehouse and product-level stock values. However, the warehouse totals are based on repeated inventory observations over time.

Therefore, cumulative stock values such as the ZMW 8.86B recorded for Lusaka Central Warehouse should not be interpreted as a single point-in-time balance.

For operational reporting, inventory should be measured using an appropriate snapshot date or an average inventory calculation.

Quality events increased across the reporting period.

The largest event categories were:

| Event Type           | Events | Affected Quantity |
| -------------------- | -----: | ----------------: |
| Customer Return      |    640 |            82,104 |
| Quality Complaint    |    350 |            16,697 |
| Damaged Product      |    288 |            15,245 |
| Packaging Defect     |    204 |            14,517 |
| Manufacturing Defect |    171 |             8,524 |
| Expired Product      |    101 |            10,615 |
| Short Shipment       |     93 |             3,931 |

Customer Returns account for the largest number of events and the largest affected quantity.

At product level, Original Cornflakes recorded the highest number of quality events at 155, followed by Chocolate Biscuits at 139 and Original Wheat Cereal at 137.

The number of quality events increased materially from 2023 to 2025. However, because sales and production also increased substantially, event counts alone are not enough to conclude that quality performance deteriorated.

A useful next metric would be quality events or affected units per million units sold or produced.

The dataset is therefore structurally suitable for the portfolio analysis, subject to the modelling considerations below.

 Partial 2026 Data

The database extends to 7 February 2026. 2026 should not be compared directly with complete years.

 Inventory Snapshots

Inventory records represent observations over time. Summing snapshot balances across dates can produce misleading cumulative figures.

 Profit Reconciliation

The headline financial figures require additional reconciliation.

The supplied totals are:

 Revenue: ZMW 1,671,396,539.75
 Cost: ZMW 1,178,786,862.17
 Reported Profit: ZMW 539,868,152.26

Revenue minus cost equals approximately ZMW 492.61M, which differs from the reported profit by approximately ZMW 47.26M.

The underlying `profit` calculation should therefore be checked before presenting the reported 32.30% margin as a formal financial measure.

 14. Areas for Further Analysis

The current dataset supports several useful extensions:

1. Measure quality events relative to units sold or produced.
2. Compare production shortfalls against downtime by factory and month.
3. Calculate inventory turnover using sales and point-in-time inventory.
4. Examine customer concentration using revenue shares by account.
5. Compare channel growth across the three-year period.
6. Analyse whether product-level margins change over time.
7. Compare purchasing growth against production and revenue growth.
8. Reconcile the profit calculation and document the final financial definition used in Power BI.
